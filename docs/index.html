The ATTEND study collects clinical records, radiology imaging, IHC, and WES-derived genomic features (TMB, MSI, HRD, aneuploidy, and a per-variant MAF) on the same patients. These reports link the modalities onto a shared patient ID, assemble a per-patient master table, and analyse it.

There is no build order. The join lives in code/build_master.R, and every report calls get_master(), which reads the master table when it exists and builds it when it does not. Any report can be the first one knitted, and wflow_build() with no arguments is always correct.

Each report opens with a four-line scope contract: the question, the cohort, what it reads, and what it deliberately leaves to another report. They are numbered in reading order, 00 to 12.

00 — Methods carries every justification in the pipeline, and the shared definitions each report links to rather than repeating: the two TMB definitions, the aneuploidy classes, what “MMR-deficient only” means, the highlight groups, and how the master is built. Operational recipes — running GISTIC on the cluster, classification alternatives — live in runbooks/.

Integration and QC

  • 01 — Data integration and quality control · all patients Part 1 translates three ID spaces onto one patient key, collapses each modality to one row per patient, and writes the master. Part 2 audits what that cost: unmapped records, coverage per dataset, the UpSet of who has which modality, and value sanity. Part 3 asks whether the automated FlowPath quantification agrees with the pathologist’s eye — a measurement-validation question, so it sits with the other checks on the inputs rather than beside the biology it feeds.

Clinical outcome

  • 02 — Survival · all patients Every Kaplan–Meier and Cox model in the pipeline: treatment arm within panels defined by aneuploidy × MMR, then TMB, HRD, MSI, and immune content.
  • 03 — Covariate distributions by MMR status · all patients ECDF and Kolmogorov–Smirnov per continuous covariate, plus the aneuploidy score itself contrasted between MMR-deficient and -proficient tumours.

Aneuploidy and the tumour microenvironment

  • 04 — Aneuploidy in MMR-deficient tumours · MMRd only Part 1 takes aneuploidy against mutation status, one gene at a time. Part 2 takes it against FlowPath cell-type composition under three normalizations.
  • 05 — Treatment response and cell-type composition · all patients + MMRd The composition panels of report 04 Part 2, re-keyed on treatment response — a 6-month landmark on the survival endpoint, with patients censored before the landmark reported as unclassifiable rather than counted as failures. Every figure is drawn for the whole cohort and for the MMR-deficient stratum, and one interaction model tests whether they differ.

Mutation burden and molecular class

  • 06 — Tumour mutational burden and the ancestry correction · all patients Part 1 asks whether TMB resolves the hypermutator population as a genuine second mode. Part 2 draws TMB by aneuploidy once per MMR stratum. Part 3 replicates the whole battery in TCGA UCEC, which is tumour-normal paired. Exactly two definitions are ever reported: the upstream tumour-only score, and the same score after the Nassar ancestry recalibration.
  • 07 — Molecular classification: the TCGA cascade and ProMisE · all patients Two classifications on the same patients, drawn side by side. The 2013 TCGA cascade (Kandoth et al.) clusters GISTIC thresholded peaks with Euclidean distance and Ward.D2 linkage, sweeps k = 2–8 on one chromosome heatmap, and calls serous-like directionally. Beside it, ProMisE / WHO-ESGO — POLEmut > MMRd > p53abn > NSMP, the algorithm WHO 2020, ESGO/ESTRO/ESP and FIGO 2023 staging actually specify — with POLE screened by exonuclease-domain hotspot rather than assumed absent, and every patient it cannot classify reported as unclassifiable rather than defaulted into NSMP. Both calls annotate the Fig-1a heatmap, so a disagreement reads as a vertical mismatch.
  • 08 — TP53 and the gene panels · all patients The genes report 07 uses as single binary markers, looked at properly: oncoplots, the configured MMR and Wnt panels, OncodriveCLUST positional clustering, and the TCGA Fig. 2d mutation-frequency comparison across the aneuploidy × MMR subgroups.

Copy number, and the external reference

  • 09 — TCGA UCEC 2013, Fig. 1a on TCGA’s own data · TCGA UCEC 2013 Report 07’s figure, built the same way, on the cohort where the right answer is published. Clusters TCGA’s own published GISTIC peaks — no parameter guess, no GISTIC run, no liftover — and scores the recomputed partition against CNA_CLUSTER_K4 with an adjusted Rand index. Also draws what the 2013 paper could not: aneuploidy against its four SCNA clusters, since that score is retrofitted from PanCancer-Atlas.
  • 10 — Recurrent copy number in aneuploidy-high tumours · all patients, then MMRd-high vs MMRp-high Two questions, in order. Are there recurrent CNAs in MMRd aneuploidy-high tumours? — per-peak recurrence inside that stratum alone, with Wilson intervals, against a cohort-wide baseline. If so, are they the same ones that recur in MMRp aneuploidy-high tumours? — the mirror plot, the pre-specified TCGA locus panel (confirmatory, Holm), the data-driven panel whose selection is nested inside the permutation (exploratory, and permanently so), peak-set overlap across the five GISTIC runs, and leave-one-out stability.
  • 11 — MMRd aneuploidy: metastatic vs primary · MMRd only, both cohorts Does aneuploidy substructure MMR-deficient tumours in metastatic disease (ATTEND) in a way that is absent from primary disease (TCGA UCEC)? Five parts: whether the split is present in each cohort, whether MMRd aneuploidy is absolutely higher in metastatic disease, how many MMRd tumours are aneuploidy-high in each, whether TMB tracks aneuploidy within an MMR stratum, and the TCGA MMRd survival split — including why it cannot decide anything. This is the report attend_cohort_cols exists for: TCGA is the external reference, so it takes the reference grey and only ATTEND carries a hue.
  • 12 — Aneuploidy, immune infiltrate and PD-L1 · all patients The Fig-1a copy-number heatmap with its columns split by the four MMR × aneuploidy groups instead of by copy-number clusters, then the pathologist’s immune calls against aneuploidy: immune infiltrate (IC %) by AS class, pooled and within each MMR group, and against the continuous score; and the two banded PD-L1 calls (immune cells, tumour cells) as ordered categories — the score across bands as a trend, and AS class × band with Fisher’s exact test.

Conventions

Shared definitions are written once, in 00-methods, and linked from the reports that use them rather than rendered into each one. Reports show derivations and results, and carry no takeaway or interpretation sections: conclusions belong in the manuscript, where they can be revised as the data changes.

Every chunk that produces a figure or table is preceded by a derivation note saying what is shown, where it comes from in the raw data, and which operations change its interpretation.

code/tests/test_rmd_style.R enforces the structure and code/tests/test_rmd_parse.R checks every chunk parses. Both are base R, so they run without tidyverse or the cohort.

Configuration lives in one place

  • code/build_master.R — the join, the crosswalk assembly, and get_master().
  • code/attend_harmonise.R — ID spaces, the two crosswalks, the collapse-to-patient rule.
  • code/attend_classes.R — column names (attend_cols), thresholds, class derivation, gene panels, the TMB definitions and labels, and the CNV/TCGA-cascade config.
  • code/attend_plots.R — the one palette (Tol vibrant), attend_theme(), attend_box(), and the highlight overlay with its crosswalk-aware id resolution.
  • code/attend_ihc.R — FlowPath IHC metric definitions (reports 02, 04, 05, 06).
  • code/attend_scna.R — GISTIC peak union, panel score, permutation and LOO stability.
  • code/attend_bimodality.R — tmb_long(), tmb_battery(), and the bimodality measures.
